## Kenya Raises Fuel Prices as Petrol, Diesel and Kerosene Costs Climb

Kenyans are set to pay more for fuel following a new price review by the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA), which took effect on Thursday, 15 October 2026.

Under the latest review, Super Petrol increased by KSh 12.93 per litre, diesel by KSh 9.90, and kerosene by KSh 28.49. The new prices will remain in force until 14 November 2026.

In Nairobi, Super Petrol is retailing at a maximum of KSh 226.96 per litre, while diesel costs KSh 227.76 and kerosene KSh 219.87.

### Why have fuel prices increased?

EPRA has attributed the increase to higher international petroleum costs, which have raised the cost of importing refined fuel into Kenya. Disruptions and instability in the Middle East have added pressure to international oil markets and shipping costs.

The regulator reported that the average landed cost of imported Super Petrol rose from US$874.26 per cubic metre in August to US$977.55 in September 2026.

### How could the increase affect Kenyans?

Higher fuel prices could put additional pressure on transport operators, motorists and households. Businesses that depend on fuel-powered transport may also face higher operating costs, potentially affecting the prices consumers pay for goods and services.

Kerosene users may feel particular pressure because it recorded the largest per-litre increase in the latest review.

### What happens next?

The new maximum retail prices apply from 15 October to 14 November 2026. Consumers and businesses will be watching the next EPRA review for any changes as international petroleum costs evolve.
